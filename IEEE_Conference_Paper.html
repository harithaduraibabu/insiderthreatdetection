Abstract—Insider threats represent one of the most critical vulnerabilities in modern enterprise cybersecurity because malicious operators already possess authorized credentials and routine access privileges. While User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA) systems leverage unsupervised anomaly detectors, such as Isolation Forests, they confront a fundamental operational dilemma in dynamic environments. Static detection models fail to accommodate benign operational evolution, causing excessive false positive rates. Conversely, naive adaptive models employing exponential moving average (EMA) updates are vulnerable to adversarial concept drift—a gradual deception and baseline poisoning strategy where adversaries incrementally escalate anomalous activities to blend into the shifting baseline. In this paper, we propose an Adaptive Behavioral Deception-Resistant Insider Threat Detection framework powered by Counterfactual Digital Twins (CDT). Our architecture establishes a privacy-preserving behavioral digital twin for each authorized entity, capturing 6-dimensional orthogonal endpoint telemetry verified at the operating system kernel level (via Win32 GetDriveTypeW). The digital twin models expected normative behavior against an unsupervised Isolation Forest (T = 100) and tracks multi-vector divergence. When cumulative feature deviation crosses an empirical statistical threshold (∑ |Zj| ≥ 4.5), an automated persistence lock freezes the counterfactual baseline, permanently preventing adversary-induced model poisoning while preserving legitimate drift adaptation and enabling counterfactual “What-If” scenario evaluations. Rigorous empirical validation over 30 independent trials across four drift stress levels confirms that under severe adversarial drift (ΔVH), the proposed counterfactual framework sustains an F1-score of 0.968 ± 0.023 and a detection rate of 96.1%, compared to 0.503 achieved by the naive adaptive baseline (p = 0.0001, paired t-test). Furthermore, the architecture complies strictly with GDPR Article 5 by inspecting only non-invasive endpoint operational metadata.

Keywords—Insider Threat Detection, Counterfactual Digital Twins, User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA), Concept Drift, Isolation Forest, Adversarial Poisoning, Endpoint Telemetry.

I. Introduction

Modern enterprise cybersecurity perimeters face unprecedented risks from insider threat vectors. Recent cybersecurity industry surveys indicate that insider incidents have risen by more than 44% across global sectors, with annual containment and incident recovery expenditures exceeding $15 million per organization [1]. Unlike external adversaries who must identify and breach perimeter defenses, malicious insiders—encompassing disgruntled employees, compromised service identities, and internal data brokers—operate from within the trust boundary. They possess legitimate cryptographic credentials, authorized system accounts, and routine access to proprietary databases and file repositories [2].

Because an insider's malicious operations closely mimic legitimate administrative or business workflows, legacy perimeter defenses—including next-generation firewalls, signature-driven endpoint protection platforms, and rigid threshold alerts—fail to detect sophisticated internal attacks [2]. To bridge this defense gap, enterprise security operations have increasingly adopted User and Entity Behavior Analytics (UEBA) frameworks. UEBA systems model historical employee activity using unsupervised machine learning algorithms, notably Isolation Forests [4], which isolate anomalies efficiently without requiring pre-labeled attack corpora.

Nevertheless, operational deployments of UEBA encounter a severe structural conflict termed the adaptation-poisoning dilemma [5]. In genuine enterprise environments, employee working patterns fluctuate continuously as project assignments shift, organizational restructuring occurs, and seasonal workloads intensify. A strictly static anomaly detection engine that locks baseline parameters at deployment cannot accommodate benign operational evolution, resulting in an unsustainable volume of false positive alarms that overwhelms Tier-1 Security Operations Center (SOC) analysts.

Conversely, if the analytics engine deploys a naive adaptive update rule—such as incrementally recalculating feature means through an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) or sliding observation window—it introduces an insidious vulnerability known as adversarial concept drift or a “boiling-frog” poisoning attack [6]. A patient insider can deliberately pace their illicit actions, exfiltrating small increments of sensitive files and network payloads over weeks or months. Under naive adaptation, each subtle increment shifts the detector's normative mean vector upward, steadily training the model to accept high-volume exfiltration as normal background behavior. When the critical mass of data is finally transferred, the poisoned model treats the event as benign, causing catastrophic detection failure [8].

To resolve this challenge, this paper introduces an end-to-end Counterfactual Digital Twin-Driven Deception-Resistant Insider Threat Detection Framework. The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:

  1. Kernel-Verified Endpoint Telemetry Sensor: We implement a low-overhead host telemetry agent that captures authentic process life cycles, delta egress bytes, and physical removable storage attachments verified directly through operating system kernel calls (GetDriveTypeW), eliminating synthetic noise from mapped network drives.
  2. Counterfactual Digital Twin Behavioral Modeling: We formulate a digital twin architecture that synthesizes normative behavior and tracks standardized multi-attribute divergence (Dt) over an orthogonal 6-dimensional representation. When composite drift crosses an empirical statistical threshold (Dt ≥ 4.5), the twin's persistence lock halts adversarial baseline poisoning while preserving legitimate drift adaptation and enabling counterfactual “What-If” evaluations.
  3. Comprehensive 30-Trial Empirical Validation: We perform extensive statistical evaluation over 30 independent multi-day simulation trials across four drift stress levels (ΔL, ΔM, ΔH, ΔVH). Paired two-tailed t-tests (p < 0.001) confirm that the proposed counterfactual framework delivers statistically significant resilience against poisoning compared to naive adaptive architectures.
  4. Privacy-Preserving and Explainable Architecture: In strict accordance with GDPR Article 5 and CCPA standards, our methodology collects zero document body contents, keystroke logs, or desktop captures, while providing transparent feature attribution narratives for SOC analysts.

II. Related Work

A. Insider Threat Detection and UEBA Architectures

Traditional insider threat detection relied on signature-based Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) and deterministic Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) correlation rules [2]. While effective against known syntax patterns, rule-based systems are brittle against novel or subtle insider evasion tactics. Modern security paradigms have therefore shifted toward data-driven UEBA architectures leveraging machine learning [3].

Supervised classifiers often struggle in operational settings due to extreme class imbalance and the lack of labeled ground-truth insider attack records. Consequently, unsupervised techniques—including autoencoders, One-Class Support Vector Machines (OC-SVM), and tree ensembles—have become standard [7]. Liu et al. introduced the Isolation Forest algorithm [4], demonstrating that anomalies are structurally susceptible to isolation via random recursive hyper-plane partitioning. Isolation Forests achieve linear computational complexity O(n log n) and minimal memory footprint, making them uniquely suited for high-throughput host telemetry processing. Recent extensions incorporate deep representations [9]; however, these approaches typically assume static feature distributions and remain vulnerable to gradual baseline shifts.

B. Concept Drift and Adversarial Baseline Poisoning

Concept drift denotes unpredictable variations in statistical data properties over time (Pt(X, y) ≠ Pt+1(X, y)) [5]. In cybersecurity telemetry, drift manifests across two distinct categories: benign operational drift (e.g., changes in team deliverables or working hours) and adversarial concept drift (deliberate evasion tactics where adversaries pollute learning parameters) [6].

Kloft and Laskov provided foundational theoretical analyses of online learning vulnerabilities under poisoning attacks, proving that unconstrained parameter updates inevitably guarantee bounded evasion for persistent adversaries [8]. Existing mitigation strategies have explored dual-memory ensembles, decaying sliding windows, and statistical drift detectors such as DDM and EDDM [5]. Nevertheless, conventional drift detectors are primarily tuned to detect sudden distribution shifts, often failing to differentiate multi-vector coordinated exfiltration from benign single-feature drift. Our work addresses this gap by introducing an explicit composite Z-score persistence lock coupled directly with an unsupervised isolation ensemble.

III. System Architecture and Threat Model

A. Threat Model and Operational Assumptions

We consider a realistic enterprise operational scenario involving a semi-honest or rogue insider operating an authorized workstation. The insider possesses valid directory credentials, administrative execution privileges on their local machine, and routine read access to proprietary project repositories. The adversary possesses basic knowledge of enterprise security auditing and purposefully seeks to exfiltrate confidential intellectual property without triggering static volumetric threshold alarms.

The attacker adopts a “low-and-slow” exfiltration pacing strategy: transferring files in modest batches during off-hours, staging compressed archives in user-level directories, and connecting physical USB mass storage devices. Under conventional adaptive UEBA, such gradual activity aims to gradually shift normative behavioral baselines over several weeks.

IV. Mathematical Formulation and Methodology

A. Standardized Behavioral Feature Vector

At each discrete operational day t, the aggregated telemetry for an observed entity is projected into a normalized 6-dimensional feature vector xt ∈ ℝ6:

xt = [fhour, fdur, fapp, ffile, fusb, fnet]T (1)

where fhour ∈ [0.0, 23.9] is the initial session login timestamp in decimal hours, fdur ∈ [0, ∞) denotes total active host session duration in minutes, fapp ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4} indicates the highest process execution risk tier observed, ffile ∈ ℕ0 counts total modified or accessed filesystem objects, fusb ∈ {0, 1, 2} reflects physical USB attachment status (0 = none, 1 = authorized peripheral, 2 = removable mass storage), and fnet ∈ [0, ∞) measures cumulative outbound network egress in megabytes (MB).

B. Unsupervised Isolation Forest Anomaly Scoring

The reference anomaly detector is trained on an initial historical calibration set of n = 600 normal operational vectors. An ensemble of T = 100 isolation trees is constructed through recursive axis-aligned hyper-plane partitioning of feature subsets [4]. For an observation vector x, the anomaly score s(x, n) is calculated as:

s(x, n) = 2−𝔼(h(x)) / c(n) (2)

where h(x) denotes the path length required to isolate instance x in tree τi, 𝔼(h(x)) is the expectation of path lengths across all 100 trees, and c(n) = 2(ln(n − 1) + 0.5772156649) − 2(n − 1)/n is the average path length of unsuccessful searches in a Binary Search Tree over sample size n. The score is mapped to an anomaly index Sanomaly = s(x, n) × 100 ∈ [0, 100].

C. Multi-Attribute Standardized Behavioral Drift Metric

Let μ0 and σ0 denote baseline mean and standard deviation vectors established during calibration. For each feature dimension j ∈ {1, ..., 6} on day t, standardized deviation is computed via Z-score transformation:

Zt, j = |xt, j − μt, j| / (σt, j + ε) (3)

where ε = 1.0 × 10−6 provides numerical stability. The composite multi-attribute behavioral drift magnitude Dt is formulated as:

Dt = ∑j=16 |Zt, j| (4)

D. The Persistence Drift-Lock Defense Mechanism

Under conventional adaptive anomaly detection, moving baseline parameters update continuously via Exponential Moving Average: μt+1 = (1 − α)μt + αxt (α = 0.15). If an adversary slowly elevates exfiltration volume, μt shifts upward, normalizing the attack. To neutralize this poisoning vector, we define a binary persistence lock state ≪t ∈ {0, 1} governed by empirical threshold τ = 4.5:

≪t = 1   if Dt ≥ 4.5   or   ≪t−1 = 1;    0   otherwise. (5)

When ≪t = 1, parameter updates are strictly halted (μt+1 = μt, σt+1 = σt), freezing the baseline against malicious assimilation. Concurrently, composite threat risk is evaluated as:

R(t) = min(100, 0.50 · Sanomaly + 50.0 · (Dt / 3.5) + ∑ Pk) (6)

with targeted penalties: Pusb = +25 for physical USB mass storage, Ptime = +20 for off-hours activity (<06:00 or >22:00 hrs), and Pproc = +20 for anonymizer tools.

V. Experimental Evaluation and Results

A. Experimental Design and Stress Levels

To evaluate the resilience of the proposed framework against adversarial concept drift, we conduct N = 30 independent empirical simulation trials across four distinct drift stress levels. Each trial spans a multi-week observation horizon comprising a 3-day baseline stabilization epoch (d0 = 3) followed by an 11-day incremental attack escalation epoch: Low Drift (+5 files/day, +20 MB/day), Medium Drift (+15 files/day, +60 MB/day), High Drift (+30 files/day, +120 MB/day), and Very High Drift (+50 files/day, +250 MB/day with off-hours scheduling and USB staging). We evaluate three competing models: Static Baseline (α = 0), Adaptive Blind EMA (α = 0.15, no drift lock), and the proposed Drift-Aware model (τ = 4.5).

C. Quantitative Results and Statistical Analysis

The experimental results in Table I demonstrate that under Low Drift, all three models maintain stable operational efficacy. However, as adversarial drift escalates to High and Very High intensity, the Adaptive (Blind EMA) detector degrades precipitously: recall collapses to 37.0% and F1-score plummets to 0.503, accompanied by 6.93 ± 0.99 false negatives. In stark contrast, the proposed Drift-Aware model sustains an F1-score of 0.968 ± 0.023 and 96.1% detection rate with a detection delay of exactly 1.00 ± 0.00 days.

To establish whether performance improvements are statistically significant, we perform two-tailed paired t-tests across N = 30 trials (α = 0.05): t = d̄ / (sd / √N). As detailed in Table III, gains in F1-score, detection rate, and detection delay are statistically significant across Medium, High, and Very High drift intensities (p < 0.001). Under severe poisoning, t = 31.42 (p = 0.0001) for F1-score, confirming with extreme confidence that the performance divergence is statistically robust.

VI. Privacy Preservation and SOC Explainability

Workplace insider threat monitoring often encounters profound regulatory and ethical tensions. Invasive surveillance techniques—such as keystroke logging, email body inspection, and periodic desktop screen capture—impose severe compliance liabilities under GDPR Article 5 (data minimization) and CCPA [10]. Our framework addresses these tensions through a privacy-by-design architecture. Telemetry acquisition operates strictly on abstract system metadata: process names, execution durations, byte counters, and hardware peripheral types. At no point are file contents, cryptographic keys, clipboard buffers, or private communications ingested or stored.

Furthermore, our system incorporates an Explainable AI (XAI) attribution service [11] that decodes mathematical Z-score anomalies into actionable, plain-language triage summaries for SOC analysts (e.g., “Anomalous off-hours login at 02:15 hrs combined with physical USB flash mount [Win32 Type 2] and a 2,850 MB network egress burst exceeding baseline by 5.8σ”). This contextual transparency reduces mean time to triage (MTTT) and empowers analysts to distinguish malicious actions from operational blunders without compromising employee privacy.

VII. Discussion and Operational Implications

The experimental results underscore three pivotal operational principles for modern enterprise security architectures:

  1. Naive Online Adaptation is Structurally Flawed: Continuously adapting anomaly baselines without drift verification creates an open attack surface that patient adversaries can exploit to evade detection entirely.
  2. Orthogonal Multi-Vector Statistical Verification: Because genuine benign drift typically alters only one or two feature dimensions simultaneously (e.g., occasional overtime during deadlines), tracking composite standardized drift (∑ |Z| ≥ 4.5) accurately differentiates benign drift from coordinated multi-stage attacks.
  3. Zero False Alarm Penalty: Crucially, the Drift-Aware detector incurs no false positive penalty during normal operations, maintaining an average of less than 0.6 false alarms per trial, ensuring that baseline locking does not induce analyst alert fatigue.

VIII. Conclusion and Future Work

This paper presented an Adaptive Behavioral Deception-Resistant Insider Threat Detection framework powered by Counterfactual Digital Twins (CDT) to resolve the adaptation-poisoning dilemma in User and Entity Behavior Analytics. By combining OS kernel-level telemetry acquisition (GetDriveTypeW) with an unsupervised Isolation Forest governed by a standardized Z-score persistence lock (Dt ≥ 4.5), the system establishes an invariant digital twin that effectively prevents adversarial baseline poisoning during prolonged exfiltration campaigns. Empirical evaluation across 30 independent experimental trials demonstrated that the proposed counterfactual framework sustains an F1-score of 0.968 ± 0.023 under severe concept drift, significantly outperforming naive adaptive models (F1 = 0.503, p = 0.0001). Future research directions include formulating reinforcement learning policies for autonomous drift threshold tuning (τ) and extending the architecture to federated multi-tenant endpoint environments.
